Made for everyday memories

Capture naturally

Speak or type a note when inspiration strikes. When your hands are busy, turn on Handsfree and say “Xayra” followed by your thought. It listens only while Handsfree is on and Xayra is open on your screen, ignores speech without “Xayra”, and turns off when you leave the app.

Ask your notes

Ask a question and Xayra answers from your notes, on your phone. It checks each answer against your notes before showing it and lists the notes it checked, so you can open the originals. If it can’t verify an answer, it says so and shows those notes instead.

Search your words

Type a word to find it in Archive, the full-screen Record view or To-Do. Join words with + to find entries that contain all of them, in any order: soccer+eli finds notes that mention both.

Your data, your choice

Notes are stored in an encrypted database on your phone. Google Drive backup is optional and encrypted, and Extra Privacy can also lock backups with a recovery code that isn’t uploaded. Calendar connections are optional too.

Privacy is part of the design.

Your notes and questions aren't sent to a remote AI service. Some features, including subscriptions and optional Google connections, rely on external services. Read the full details in our Privacy Policy.

THE INSPIRATION & THE SCIENCE

Four decades of memories. A lifetime of remembering.

For over 40 years, my father has written down the key events of his day in a journal.

And every now and then, he goes back and reads them.

What fascinates me isn’t just the thousands of moments he’s preserved. It’s how vividly he can recall events from years, sometimes decades, ago. To me, he has the sharpest memory in the room when it comes to remembering our past.

I don’t know how much of that is because of his journaling. But watching him revisit those pages made me wonder:

What if more of us could build the habit of capturing and rediscovering our everyday experiences?

That question helped inspire Xayra.

And there’s science behind the curiosity.

Research into autobiographical memory suggests that revisiting recorded experiences can help people recall more details about those events. In one study, older adults who replayed recordings of everyday experiences recalled significantly more event-specific details than they did for comparison events.

It’s an intriguing finding, and one that reinforces the value of making personal experiences easier to preserve and revisit.

Xayra makes that habit simpler, more searchable and private.

Read the original study (Martin et al., PNAS, 2022)

My father’s experience is a personal observation, not scientific evidence of causation. Research on recording and revisiting experiences does not establish that Xayra improves memory, cognitive health or wellbeing. Xayra has not been clinically evaluated for those outcomes.
